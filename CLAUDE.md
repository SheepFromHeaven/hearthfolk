# MineNow

NeoForge 26.3 mod (NeoForge `26.3.0.101`, mod loader plugin `net.neoforged.moddev`). Group/mod id: `com.sheepfromheaven.minenow`. Java 25.

## Vision

Vanilla villagers that get better at their existing professions in the farms and workshops the player builds. No custom
mob, no town banner, no screens. Vanilla already provides houses (beds), workplaces (workstations), growth (breeding)
and schedules; we only add behaviours to the villager brain. Professions are added one at a time, farmer first.

## Source layout (`src/main/java/com/sheepfromheaven/minenow/`)

- `MineNow.java` — mod entry point, event wiring, gametest registration
- `MineNowConfig` — per-world switches (a `LOCAL` config: this FancyModLoader has `LOCAL`/`SYNCED`/`CLIENT`/`STARTUP`, no `SERVER`), file `minenow-local.toml`: `villagersSummonGolems` (default false, see `golemsmith/`) and `travellingMerchants` (default true, see `villager/Exports`). Tests that flip a switch restore it in a `finally` and run in the `config` test environment, so the flip never reaches tests in other batches
- `villager/BrainHook` — adds our behaviours to every adult villager's brain (re-applied whenever vanilla rebuilds it), after `VillagerBrains` rebuilds it from vanilla's packages: MEET and IDLE without `TradeWithVillager` (no handing food/goods to each other; goods move by trading), farmers with `FarmerWork`'s WORK package, CORE with `PanicOrFight` in place of vanilla's panic trigger (plus a FIGHT activity for it), and `WalkToPoi` in place of vanilla's walks back to the job site, bed and meeting point
- `villager/WalkToPoi` — keeps a villager's job site, bed and meeting point on a walk across the village. Vanilla's `SetWalkTargetFromBlockMemory` gives the place up once `CANT_REACH_WALK_TARGET_SINCE` is over a minute old, but every walk sets that (a walk to anything past a villager's 16-block follow range only ever gets a partial path), and beyond 100 blocks (150 for the bed) it gives the place up outright. The job site is never given up for being out of reach (`WalkToPoi.keeping`), only once its block is gone, which is checked wherever the villager is (vanilla's `ValidateNearbyPoi` only within 16 blocks); two villagers of one trade holding the same block is still settled by vanilla's `PoiCompetitorScan`. For the bed and meeting point only failing to reach the place itself, from within 16 blocks of it, counts: a streak of failed walks to it lasts while that timestamp stays the same, so whatever clears it (a miner down its mine) ends the streak as in vanilla. Villagers have vanilla's default follow range of 16, so no path is ever planned further than that: anything further is reached by partial paths
- `villager/ChooseMeetingPoint` — each late afternoon a villager drops its bell and claims the nearest one it can reach from where it is (48 blocks, within the bell's 6-block reach), so meeting points vary day to day; the bell it claims makes it one of that bell's village (`Villages`)
- `villager/Villages` — a village grows out of its bells: every villager's claimed bell, bed, job site and chests are its places (the anchors), a villager's places belong together, and places within 32 blocks of each other across (48 up or down) join up, whoever's they are, so beds and workshops between two bells make them one village however far apart the bells are. Each group of places with a bell in it is a village, its area everything within 16 blocks of its places; a villager belongs to the village its places are in (none if they hold no bell), or one with no places to the village it stands in. Joining uses a 32-block grid, so it stays cheap with hundreds of villagers. Rebuilt every 100 ticks from the villagers loaded in the level (sooner for a villager that claimed a bell it's not listed under yet), never saved. Trading and commissions stay inside a village; the alarm covers all of it (`Alarms`). Gametests are laid out 5 blocks apart, so two tests with bells in one batch are one village: each test with a bell has an environment of its own (`village_1` to `village_16`, work hours), and those needing two villages use `structure/field_72x8x24.nbt`
- `villager/HomeStorage` — each adult villager claims one chest within 8 blocks of its bed (custom `home_storage` POI on chests), locks it for players, releases it on death/conversion; right-clicking it says whose it is. Breaking it isn't theft (the player may be rebuilding): its contents drop as the villager's (`Belongings`: vanilla's item target keeps players from picking them up, no despawning), the villager gathers them back (`Belongings.Gather`, any adult while awake) and its unloading puts them in its next chest; once the villager dies or zombifies they're ordinary items. A hopper or hopper minecart swallowing them is theft by its placer (`Belongings.onEntityLeave`, the owner read from the item's vanilla target since attachments are gone by then; `BREAK_GOSSIP` per stack); its own gathering clears the target first. `ModVillagers` holds the POI type and the `storage`/`placed_by` data attachments
- `villager/Workshop` — workshop storage: a working villager also claims the chests within 6 blocks of its job block (2 up or down), the nearest first, as many as its level (novice 1, master 5), checked every 2 s with the home chest (`HomeStorage.onEntityTick`). Only plain chests (`home_storage` POI), so a trapped chest is always the player's; a chest is one villager's home chest or one workshop's, never both. Locked, shown as theirs, broken and drained like a home chest. Trade goods are unloaded there and food and emeralds at home (`Workshop.personal`; `Unloading.unloadTarget` picks the chest, `deposit` only puts in what belongs; with only one kind of chest everything goes there, and trade goods go home when the workshop is full); after unloading at one, a villager with goods left for the other finishes the round. Economy counts, takes and gives over home and workshop chests (`Storage.chests` outside the package). The chests go with the job site: released when the job's lost. `villager/Walking.towards`: a behaviour walking further than 9 blocks from its job site (to a home chest) re-pins its walk target, since vanilla's work package keeps pulling villagers back
- `villager/Economy` — a villager's goods are its inventory plus its home chest and workshop chests: counting, taking, giving, and the one-time starting purse (6) granted when it first takes a job (`BrainHook`, so children bring money into the village when they grow up and get a job), once per villager for its whole life: a new job brings nothing, and the `purse_granted` flag survives zombifying and curing (`copyOnDeath`, copied on conversion). When buying from players or other villagers a villager never goes below its `reserve`: 2 emeralds food money plus the price of the cheapest tool of its trade (a villager with no tool may spend down to the food money on one, and likewise one of a fuel-burning trade with no fuel on fuel, and food may use the food money), so a crafter without customers pauses with full shelves instead of going broke; every supply offer shares that one purse, so after each purchase the others are capped to what's left. The other sources of new money are the miner digging emerald ore and exports (`villager/Exports`). `GameTestHelpers.spawnEmployed` leaves the purse out (tests hand out the emeralds they need, `giveStartingPurse` adds it)
- `villager/WorkXp` — levelling through work: `WorkXp.award(villager, xp)` adds vanilla villager experience and runs vanilla's level-up (new trades, via access transformers on `shouldIncreaseLevel`/`increaseMerchantCareer`), so skills scale with work, not only with trading with players. Capped at `DAILY_CAP` (24) per villager per game day (`work_xp_day`/`work_xp_today` attachments), about ten days of steady work from novice to master; none for unemployed or nitwits. Amounts are decimal and carried over (`work_xp_progress`) until they add up to a whole point; the per-action constants live in `WorkXp`: 0.05 per block dug, 0.1 per crop harvested or road block paved, 0.2 per log felled, 0.5 per furnace output collected or flower bone-mealed, 1 per catch, sheep sheared, animal culled, item crafted (`CraftAtWorkstation`), blade restored, piece dyed, armor piece displayed or archery round. A new work behaviour should call it too. `GameTestHelpers.spawnEmployed` villagers have used up their day's experience so a level a test sets stays put (`allowWorkXp` gives it back)
- `villager/TradeTable` — per-profession prices and level unlocks from `data/<ns>/villager_trades/<profession>.json` (reloads with `/reload`); every price is one decimal number of emeralds per item (0.05 is 20 for an emerald, 3 is one for three; `villager/Price` turns it into the smallest whole-emerald deal, the same for players and villagers); `tools` are bought when the villager has none, `produce` is sold at its `price`, `supplies` are bought at their `price` in that deal's batch while the villager holds less than one batch in its goods (chests included, or a batch bigger than `max` went to the chest and it bought again), and it keeps up to `max` on it, taking supplies (and a tool of its trade, when it has none on it) back out of the chest when it unloads (`Unloading.restock`), since its work only uses what it carries; a tool of its trade it's given with full pockets and none on it goes in its pockets, another stack going to the chest (`Economy.give`), `default_produce` prices anything in a set (farmer: `#c:crops`, 0.05) that has no `produce` entry, `keeps` lists goods the villager makes and works on further (kept up to `max` when unloading, never bought), and `crafts` lists items it makes at its workstation from `materials`, `count` at a time (default 1), wearing the tool of its trade `tool_uses` times (default 0), priced by `TradeTable.craftPrice`: materials (valued at its own supply price, else what it sells them for, crafts included) plus decimal `labor` plus tool wear (cheapest tool's price over its durability, per use), split over `count` and sold in the smallest whole-emerald batch like any price; things that don't stack (tools) are one item for whole emeralds, rounded up. A `produce` entry overrides a craft's price. Several entries for one item at different levels: the latest unlocked one is the recipe (`craft(item, level)`), so a level can make something cheaper; several entries at the same level are alternatives (sticks or shields from any planks), all used. A crafter keeps `stock` but never less than one sale's batch (`stockTarget`), so it always has a whole batch to sell. `TradeTable.sellPrice` is the one lookup: produce entry, then craft, then `default_produce`; a level unlocks its entries and keeps earlier ones
- `villager/ProfessionTrades` + `Unloading` + `PickUpGiftedTool` — profession-neutral: any villager whose profession has a trade file gets data-driven offers (buys a tool of its trade when it owns none, sells produce, buys supplies), keeps one tool / supplies up to `max` / seeds and food up to 16 and deposits the rest in its home chest, and picks up a tool thrown to it. A trade file's `tool_tag` (farmer `#minecraft:hoes`, fisherman `#c:tools/fishing_rod`) decides what counts as its tool, so modded tools work
- `villager/Meals` + `EatMeals` — every adult villager eats twice a day (`last_meal` attachment, 12000 ticks): food from `#minenow:villager_meals` (bread, vegetables, fruit, cooked meat and fish, ...) worth 6 nutrition, most filling first, from its inventory or home chest. A balanced diet: it alternates bread, vegetables and the like with cooked meat or fish (`last_meal_meat` attachment), eating the other kind than last time when it has a meal's worth of it
- `villager/Wealth` — wealth pays for children: a villager with 10+ emeralds in its goods and under vanilla's 12 breeding food points (items with `VILLAGER_FOOD`: bread, carrot, potato, beetroot) buys one emerald's worth (food in its chests counts, or it would buy again every time it unloads); vanilla breeding does the rest (partner, free bed)
- `villager/BuyFromVillagers` — villager-to-villager trading: a hungry villager without a meal buys the most filling food for its money (an emerald buys far more carrots than bread), a villager with money above its reserve buys some of the other kind of food for its next meal, a hungry villager with no meal and no money is given one by a neighbour holding more of a food than it could sell (`Exports.bulk`; vanilla villagers share food, here only with those who can't pay), and a wealthy one short of breeding food buys one emerald's worth from the nearest seller (any time it's awake); while it works, meets or idles (a miner is down its mine all working day, so its coal and ore could only be bought after work), a villager holding less than one batch (`count`) of a supply of its trade in its goods, with emeralds, walks to the nearest villager of its village that sells it, however far (a villager of no village trades with those within 48 blocks that belong to none either; two villages never trade), giving up after 30 s plus a second per block past 24 (`TradeTable.perEmerald`: a `produce` entry or `default_produce`, unlocked at the seller's level) and buys at the seller's player price, as many emeralds' worth as it can afford, the seller can spare (goods minus what it keeps) and fits under the supply's `max`. Goods and emeralds move through `Economy`, so home chests count. First case: the butcher buys wheat from the farmer. Trades need line of sight (no buying through glass), and a seller down a mine is skipped (buyers fell in after it). Prices are the smallest whole-emerald deal, so cheap goods come in big lots (raw copper 50 for 3 emeralds); a seller short of a whole lot sells an emerald's share instead (`lotFor`). Credit: a craftsman too broke to pay for a tool or a supply of its trade takes one lot on account from a seller with a lot to spare (not while resting), up to `CREDIT_LIMIT` (3) emeralds owed in all (`debts` attachment), and pays it back from earnings above its reserve, so a smelter with no ore or a butcher with no fuel can start. A trade that buys fuel (butcher, smelter) buys what it cooks only once it holds fuel (`waitsForFuel`), or it spent its purse on fish it could never smoke. Tool upgrades: a working villager with money above its reserve buys the best better tool of its trade a crafter has (further down its trade file's `tools`, which run worst to best, unlocked at its level), throwing its old one away (nobody buys a worn tool); a miner whose pickaxe can't harvest something in its mine (`Miner.pickaxeHoldsItBack`: ore down to its depth) takes the cheapest better one on credit, since a wooden pickaxe leaves iron and copper ore in the walls
- `villager/Commissions` — work paid for up front, so money pooled at the trades everyone buys from reaches the ones that make things: when nothing is to be bought (`BuyFromVillagers.newCommission`), a villager with money to spare (`Gear.budget`) orders a piece of gear nobody has in stock from a villager of its village that makes it (unlocked at its level), a working villager a lot of a supply of its trade nobody can spare from one that sells it, or a wealthy one has a powered mason pave the dirt path to its home; it pays the maker's price at once, so the maker can buy what it needs (farmer pays the armorer, the armorer the smelter for copper, the smelter the miner for ore). One open order per buyer. The order lives on the maker (`ModVillagers.COMMISSIONS`: buyer, item and count or path start, paid, placed) and the buyer remembers whom it paid (`COMMISSIONED`), both data attachments, so a restart loses nothing (`commission_survives_a_restart` saves and reloads both villagers). The maker crafts what's ordered on top of its stock and holds it back from other buyers, merchants and bulk (`Commissions.reserved`); the buyer collects it once the maker has it (gear goes straight on). Undelivered after `EXPIRES` (3 days) the buyer asks for its money back (a paving order only once the mason has stopped paving); an order whose buyer never came is dropped after twice that, and a buyer whose maker is gone forgets it then
- `villager/Exports` — the village's exports: goods only players would otherwise buy (`#minenow:exports`: armor, swords, shields, bows, crossbows, saddles, leather horse armor, bundles, and the golemsmith's iron bars, buckets and cauldrons). Once a minute near each player (`Exports.visitNear`, unless the `travellingMerchants` switch is off), if no wandering trader is within 96 blocks, the export goods of the villagers within 48 blocks draw a travelling merchant with `Exports.visitChance` (none under 8 emeralds' worth, rising to 50% at 64), at most one every 5 minutes, onto open ground near the villager with the most. The merchant is a wandering trader marked `TRAVELLING_MERCHANT`, named "Travelling Merchant", with two chested pack mules on leads instead of llamas (`PACK_MULE`; they leave with it, and an untamed one without a lead disappears), selling goods from afar the village can't make (`data/<ns>/travelling_merchant/*.json`, all files merged, one decimal price per item like the trade files, items of missing mods skipped; by default string, sugar cane, cocoa beans, sweet berries, honeycomb, nether wart) instead of vanilla's random trades. It walks to villagers within 24 blocks holding export goods and buys them at the villager's own price, in whole batches, with new emeralds, up to 32 per merchant; with what's left it buys bulk surplus at half price (`Exports.bulk`: anything a villager sells beyond what it keeps and beyond half a stack, so a miner buried in cobblestone or a fisherman in fish still earns, and the merchant can't empty the village of anything), which also counts towards the visit chance. Vanilla's own wandering trader stays vanilla and buys nothing
- `villager/Gear` + `PanicOrFight` — villagers buy, wear and use armor and swords. Armor goes in its armor slots, worn all the time (vanilla protection applies, a guaranteed drop on death, kept by a zombified villager), and wears down with the blows it takes like a player's (`Gear.onDamage`; vanilla never wears mob armor). A weapon is kept at its side (`SIDEARM` attachment, not goods, so it's never sold or exported) and drawn only to fight: a sword, or for archers a bow with arrows in a quiver (`QUIVER` attachment). About one villager in three is an archer for life (`ARCHER` attachment, defaulting from its UUID via `Gear.prefersBow`); weapon and arrows drop on death and conversion. Buying goes through `BuyFromVillagers` after food, tools and supplies, with what's above its reserve plus `Gear.SAVINGS` (4): the most protective armor the nearest seller can spare for an empty slot, its weapon (sword from the weaponsmith, bow from the fletcher) only once all four slots are filled, and an archer refills its quiver to 32 arrows from the fletcher once it's down to 8; the piece is handed over as it is (`Economy.takeOne`, so dyed leather keeps its colour). `PanicOrFight`: an armed villager (a sword at its side, or a bow at its side or in its goods with arrows in its quiver or goods) above a third of its health fights a hostile instead of panicking: what hurt it if that's an `Enemy`, else vanilla's nearest hostile (raiders included), in the FIGHT activity so panic, hiding and raids don't pull it away; swings like a player (`Weapons`: the item's attack damage and speed, shared with the butcher), wearing the sword, or shoots with the fletcher's aim; only starts a fight with an enemy it can see (so a remembered attacker out of sight doesn't draw it in while it goes about its day) and chases no further than 8 blocks from where the fight began (a fight restarting within 10 s keeps the first starting point), then returns to its day and puts the weapon away; melee needs line of sight too. Unarmed or badly hurt villagers panic as in vanilla. `client/VillagerArmorLayer` draws the armor (see `client/`). Tests spawning hostiles use the `hostiles` test environment so they run in their own batch: the hostile sensor reaches through the barrier walls and panics villagers in neighbouring tests
- `villager/Muster` + `HighGround` + `Sight` + `Eyes` — an alarm (a bell the villager hears, `HEARD_BELL_TIME`, or an active raid at its position) sends every armed, healthy villager to a post instead of hiding as vanilla would, in the FIGHT activity so `ReactToBell`, `SetRaidStatus` and the raid packages can't pull it away (it takes the bell memory so `ReactToBell` stops firing, and only when it found a post, else vanilla hides it). The alarm lasts 1200 ticks after the last ring, or while the raid goes on; then it returns to its schedule. Swordsmen (and archers with nowhere to climb) gather within 3 blocks of the meeting point (its claimed bell, else the nearest within 48). Archers (`Gear.shoots`: armed and not a sword at the side) take `HighGround.find` within 12 blocks of the bell, only a spot a villager can walk to (stairs and ramps, never ladders): first a post the player marked with a floor block of `#minenow:archer_posts` (hay bales), anywhere from the bell's level up, always preferred over other spots; else the best floor 3 to 12 blocks above the bell, indoors or out (roofed towers count), solid and not leaves or barrier, two free cells above, height counting double against distance, one candidate per 3x3x3 patch and 16 path checks so unreachable roofs don't crowd out a wall, 2.5 blocks from other archers' spots. A perched archer (`Muster.holdsPerch`) never walks to a hostile and doesn't panic at hostiles it can't see. What every adult villager notices is `Sight`, written into `NEAREST_HOSTILE` by `Eyes` (first in CORE, every tick, replacing vanilla's sensor result so panic, fighting and hiding all use it): only the mobs vanilla's sensor knows (its table, or the NeoForge `ACCEPTABLE_VILLAGER_DISTANCES` data map: husk and zombie 8, pillager 15, ...), at the sensor's distance plus 1.5 per block above the mob (up to 8 blocks), times 0.4 to 1 by the mob's light level (`getMaxLocalRawBrightness`, so a mob in the dark is only noticed close and a lit one from far), in line of sight; rechecked every 10 ticks per villager. Shooters reach `Sight.bowRange` (15 plus the height above the target, up to 8). `PanicOrFight.isFighting` tells `Muster` to leave the walking to the fight. Tests that ring a bell use the `bell` test environment (own batch): the bell reaches villagers within 32 blocks, through test walls, and sends the neighbours' unarmed villagers hiding. In gametests the arena has a barrier roof at y 6, which the tower tests clear
- `villager/IdleReason` + `IdleCues` + `SeekTool` + `IdleReport` — why a villager isn't working, for every profession with a trade file: `IdleReason.of` lists what stops it (no job site; no tool of its trade, from the trade file's `tool_tag`/`tools`; pockets full with no chest having room for what it must unload). No angry particles: they read as the player's bad reputation. `SeekTool` (in CORE): a working villager without its tool holds up an emerald (a copy, only when it could pay a player for a tool) and walks up to a player within 16 blocks who is within 24 of its job site, cheering (`VILLAGER_CELEBRATE`) once close; it yields to `BuyFromVillagers` when a villager nearby sells a tool it can afford, and to picking up a thrown tool, buying, eating and gathering. `PickUpGiftedTool` overrides the walk target, since vanilla's work package keeps pointing it at the job site. `IdleCues` (every 4 s per villager, only in the WORK activity with a player within 24 blocks): a head shake with vanilla's "no" sound (`Villager.setUnhappy`, via access transformer) for nowhere to unload. `/minenow villager` (and `/minenow farmer`, the farmers only) reports the nearest one: activity and the MineNow behaviour running, tool, slots, chests, the reasons; a profession adds lines through `IdleReport.addExtra` (the farmer's field, `farmer/FarmerReport`). Add a reason as an enum value plus a cue
- `villager/VillagerNames` — every villager without a custom name gets one from `data/<ns>/villager_names/*.json` (all files merged) when it joins the world; vanilla then shows it on hover and in the trade title ("Jim - Novice")
- `villager/StorageTheft` — any unexplained drop in an owned chest's contents is theft, blamed on whoever placed the hopper or hopper minecart under it. Code that takes items out of a villager's chest must call `StorageTheft.check` before and `recordContents` after, or it will be counted as theft
- `villager/Roads` + `RoadNavigation` + `StrollAlongRoads` — every villager gets a navigation (swapped in on join, via the access transformer on `Mob.navigation`) whose steps cost extra by road tier. Tiers are data: `data/<ns>/villager_road_tiers/*.json` gives a block list (tags `#minenow:villager_roads/tier_1..3`: dirt path; gravel/cobble; bricks/planks/slabs/stairs) and a cost (0.75, 0.4, 0); open ground costs 1.0 and anything cheaper counts as road. Adding a tier is a tag plus a tier file. Idle villagers also stroll to nearby road blocks. Vanilla's PathFinder weights its heuristic by 1.5, so open ground must cost well over 0.5 or detours are never explored. Landing from a jump of 2+ blocks costs 8 for 2 and four times more per extra block (`RoadNavigation.dropCost`, on the landing node, taken once), so villagers take stairs, ramps and ladders down when there are any
- `villager/FollowEmeraldHolder` — unemployed adult villagers follow a player within 10 blocks holding an emerald (not while asleep, trading, panicking or in a raid); led more than 32 blocks from their bed they release it and their meeting bell (vanilla never would) and settle anew
- `farmer/Crops` — what counts as a field crop: any `CropBlock` or `#minecraft:crops` entry (where crop mods register theirs), minus stems and `#minenow:farmer_ignores`; ripe = max age (read from the `age` property for non-CropBlocks); a seed is an item that plants such a crop (or a NeoForge `SpecialPlantable`). Planting checks the crop can survive there
- `farmer/RightClickHarvest` — before breaking a ripe crop the farmer right-clicks it through a NeoForge fake player, so right-click-harvest mods (and crops harvested that way natively) work for villagers; drops spawned at the crop or handed to the fake player go to the farmer. If nothing reacts, the farmer breaks and replants as before. `gametest/SimulatedRightClickHarvest` stands in for such a mod in tests
- `farmer/Field` — recomputed on every scan (when the farmer starts work or runs out of it); a farmer's field is the ground touching a side of its composter (corners don't count) and what connects to it: farmland plus tillable ground (`turns_into_farmland` tag) within vanilla's 4-block watering range, joined through water, surface only, within a range that grows with the farmer's level (novice 10, apprentice 13, journeyman 16, expert 20, master 24). Water is the boundary players build with; anything solid or non-replaceable on top of ground (string, slabs, fences) also cuts it out of the field
- `farmer/` — `TendFields` behaviour (harvest/replant/till its connected field, unload into the farmer's own home chest; needs a hoe, holds it, swings it while reaping for as long as the hoe's mining speed dictates, wears it down, fetches a spare from the home chest; lives in CORE right after `MoveToTargetSink` so standing still to reap isn't undone by WORK's walk back to the job site), `FarmerReport` (`/minenow farmer`: why the nearest farmer is or isn't working: activity, hoe, slots, home chest, field counts), `FarmerWork` (the farmer's WORK package: vanilla's minus `WorkAtComposter` (bread baking, seed composting), `HarvestFarmland` and `UseBonemeal`; TendFields does that work, including bone-mealing young crops; bread is a priced craft in `farmer.json` instead: 3 wheat a loaf, kept up to 32 so 16 are for sale above the food it keeps), `FarmerRules` (farmers never trample farmland) and its gametests
- `fisherman/` — `FishingWater` (any open surface water within range of the barrel, so barrels can stand on docks: 8 blocks, 3 up or down, the same at every level; shore spots are dry standable blocks beside it) `GoFishing` (walks to the nearest shore spot, casts a bobber, reels in when a fish bites, catch from vanilla's fishing loot table with Luck of the Sea, wears the rod, unloads at its home chest; in CORE like TendFields) and `VillagerBobber` (our own bobber entity, since vanilla's hook needs a player owner: flies, floats, a fish swims towards it with vanilla's V-shaped particle trail, bites; Lure shortens the wait; removes itself when GoFishing stops tending it). `FishermanSkill` is what levels improve: reflexes (a novice sometimes reacts too late and the fish gets away, a master never) and rod care (less wear per catch). `client/VillagerBobberRenderer` draws it with the line to the villager's rod. Trades in `villager_trades/fisherman.json`
- `shepherd/` — `ShearSheep` (shears adult sheep within 8 blocks of the loom, 3 up or down: walks up, holds the sheep still and shears for `ShepherdSkill.shearTicks` (novice 3 s, master 1 s), wool from vanilla's shearing loot table straight into its inventory minus what it tears (novice 40%, master none), unloads at its home chest; in CORE like TendFields). Shepherds open fence gates (`villager/OpenFenceGates`). Trades in `villager_trades/shepherd.json` (shears, any `#minecraft:wool` 18 per emerald; spins 8 wool of any colour into 16 string at its loom, for the toolsmith's rods and the fletcher's bows, which otherwise only the travelling merchant sells)
- `butcher/` — `TendHerd` keeps each kind of livestock (`#minenow:butcher_livestock` entity tag: cow, pig, chicken, rabbit; not sheep) within 8 blocks of the smoker at a steady herd (`ButcherHerd`: young count but are never culled, adults above 6 are culled, never below 2 adults, always keeps 2 young growing up with food it holds, so a full herd still yields meat); named or leashed animals are pets and never culled. Culls with its axe the way a player fights, from a player's 3-block melee reach, finishing a wounded animal before starting another (`Butchering`: as many swings as the animal's health over the axe's attack damage, one per attack cooldown, each swing wears the axe), so better axes (unlocked by level in the trade file) work faster, like the farmer's hoes; `ButcherDrops` (LivingDropsEvent) puts the drops straight into its inventory plus `ButcherSkill.extraCuts` (0 novice to 2 master, like Looting). Smokes its raw meat in its smoker with coal it holds and takes the cooked meat out (`villager/FurnaceTending`); unloads at its home chest. ## Dev-only mods

`build.gradle`'s `clientDevMods` configuration (Right Click Harvest + JamLib) is added only to the client run's classpath, for trying compatibility by hand. Never use `runtimeOnly` for such mods: that loads them in `runGameTestServer` too, which changes behaviour under test and makes CI depend on Modrinth.

## Workflow

- **TDD**: write a failing test first, then implement — a GameTest if the behavior needs a live `Level`
  (blocks, entities, ticking), a plain JUnit test under `src/test/java` only if it's pure logic that touches no
  items: on 26.3 item components are bound when datapacks load, so `new ItemStack(...)` throws "Components not bound
  yet" in unit tests. Anything with items is a gametest (they can be instant, see `better_hoes_reap_faster`). See
  `feedback_tdd_workflow` memory.
- **Verify with GameTests, not just compile**: `./gradlew runGameTestServer` (`-Ptests=minenow:name` runs one, `*` wildcards allowed) — a green compile can still hide a broken tag/behavior. Don't declare a task done on compile alone. For pure-logic changes covered by `src/test/java`, `./gradlew test` is the equivalent gate.
- `@GameTest` annotations don't register on this build; tests are wired via `RegisterEvent` + JSON (see `reference_forge_gametest_setup` memory).
- Custom block tags live in `tags/block/` (singular), not `tags/blocks/` — wrong folder fails silently.
- ARGB colors: always include the alpha byte — omitting it renders fully transparent.
- Villager AI: add `Behavior<Villager>`s to vanilla brain activities via `BrainHook`; no mixins unless a vanilla hook is truly missing. To drop a vanilla behaviour, rebuild that profession's brain from `VillagerGoalPackages` the way `FarmerWork` does (only on a fresh brain).
- Access transformers live in `META-INF/accesstransformer.cfg` (declared in `neoforge.mods.toml`); prefer one over a mixin when only visibility is in the way.
- `PathNavigation.createPath` returns null until the mob is on the ground (and never with NoAI); compute paths a few ticks after spawning in tests.
- Villagers never summon iron golems unless `villagersSummonGolems` is on (`Golemsmith.onFinalizeSpawn` cancels `MOB_SUMMONED` golems); vanilla's trigger on 26.3 only needed villagers that slept recently. `LAST_WORKED_AT_POI` is written by vanilla but never read and not even registered.
- Per-villager state that must survive reloads goes in a NeoForge data attachment, not a custom brain `MemoryModuleType`: the brain drops saved memories that aren't registered at rebuild time, and ours are only added afterwards.

## Token-cost hygiene (read before running builds/tests)

- Compile before running gametests: `./gradlew compileJava` first, `runGameTestServer` only once that's clean. A compile error inside a full gametest run produces far more noisy output than catching it standalone.
- Always add `--console=plain -q` to gradle invocations to suppress Minecraft/Forge startup spam (asset indexing, mod discovery), e.g. `./gradlew runGameTestServer --console=plain -q 2>&1 | tail -80`.
- Never dump raw `./gradlew runGameTestServer` / build output into context. Pipe through `grep`/`tail` for the failure only, e.g. `./gradlew runGameTestServer --console=plain -q 2>&1 | tail -80` or `| grep -A20 FAILED`.
- Don't `cat`/`Read` full log files under `run/logs/`, `run/crash-reports/`, or `build/`; grep for the specific error first. (These are also permission-denied for `Read` in `.claude/settings.json` — use `grep`/`tail` via Bash instead.)
- For "find X" or "where is Y defined" questions, use the `Explore` agent instead of reading many files directly in the main conversation.
- Prefer `Edit` over re-reading a file you just wrote to confirm it — the tool already errors on failure.

# MineNow

NeoForge 26.3 mod (NeoForge `26.3.0.101`, mod loader plugin `net.neoforged.moddev`). Group/mod id: `com.sheepfromheaven.minenow`. Java 25.

## Vision

Vanilla villagers that get better at their existing professions in the farms and workshops the player builds. No custom
mob, no town banner, no screens. Vanilla already provides houses (beds), workplaces (workstations), growth (breeding)
and schedules; we only add behaviours to the villager brain. Professions are added one at a time, farmer first.

## Source layout (`src/main/java/com/sheepfromheaven/minenow/`)

- `MineNow.java` — mod entry point, event wiring, gametest registration
- `villager/BrainHook` — adds our behaviours to every adult villager's brain (re-applied whenever vanilla rebuilds it), after `VillagerBrains` rebuilds it from vanilla's packages: MEET and IDLE without `TradeWithVillager` (no handing food/goods to each other; goods move by trading), farmers with `FarmerWork`'s WORK package
- `villager/ChooseMeetingPoint` — each late afternoon a villager drops its bell and claims the nearest one it can reach from where it is (48 blocks, within the bell's 6-block reach), so meeting points vary day to day
- `villager/HomeStorage` — each adult villager claims one chest within 8 blocks of its bed (custom `home_storage` POI on chests), locks it for players, releases it on death/conversion; right-clicking it says whose it is; a player breaking it is theft (negative gossip). `ModVillagers` holds the POI type and the `storage`/`placed_by` data attachments
- `villager/Economy` — a villager's goods are its inventory plus its home chest: counting, taking, giving, and the one-time starting purse (6) granted when it first takes a job (`BrainHook`, so children bring money into the village when they grow up and get a job), once per villager for its whole life: a new job brings nothing, and the `purse_granted` flag survives zombifying and curing (`copyOnDeath`, copied on conversion). When buying from players a villager never goes below its `reserve`: 2 emeralds food money plus the price of the cheapest tool of its trade (a villager with no tool may spend down to the food money on one); every supply offer shares that one purse, so after each purchase the others are capped to what's left. The other source of new money is the miner digging emerald ore. `GameTestHelpers.spawnEmployed` leaves the purse out (tests hand out the emeralds they need, `giveStartingPurse` adds it)
- `villager/TradeTable` — per-profession prices and level unlocks from `data/<ns>/villager_trades/<profession>.json` (reloads with `/reload`); every price is one decimal number of emeralds per item (0.05 is 20 for an emerald, 3 is one for three; `villager/Price` turns it into the smallest whole-emerald deal, the same for players and villagers); `tools` are bought when the villager has none, `produce` is sold at its `price`, `supplies` are bought at their `price` in that deal's batch while the villager holds at most `max` (and it keeps up to `max` when unloading), `default_produce` prices anything in a set (farmer: `#c:crops`, 0.05) that has no `produce` entry, `keeps` lists goods the villager makes and works on further (kept up to `max` when unloading, never bought), and `crafts` lists items it makes at its workstation from `materials`, sold for the materials (valued at its own supply or produce price) plus its `labor`, rounded up to whole emeralds (`TradeTable.craftPrice`); a level unlocks its entries and keeps earlier ones
- `villager/ProfessionTrades` + `Unloading` + `PickUpGiftedTool` — profession-neutral: any villager whose profession has a trade file gets data-driven offers (buys a tool of its trade when it owns none, sells produce, buys supplies), keeps one tool / supplies up to `max` / seeds and food up to 16 and deposits the rest in its home chest, and picks up a tool thrown to it. A trade file's `tool_tag` (farmer `#minecraft:hoes`, fisherman `#c:tools/fishing_rod`) decides what counts as its tool, so modded tools work
- `villager/Meals` + `EatMeals` — every adult villager eats twice a day (`last_meal` attachment, 12000 ticks): food from `#minenow:villager_meals` (bread, vegetables, fruit, cooked meat and fish, ...) worth 6 nutrition, most filling first, from its inventory or home chest
- `villager/Wealth` — wealth pays for children: a villager with 10+ emeralds in its goods and under vanilla's 12 breeding food points (items with `VILLAGER_FOOD`: bread, carrot, potato, beetroot) buys one emerald's worth; vanilla breeding does the rest (partner, free bed)
- `villager/BuyFromVillagers` — villager-to-villager trading: a hungry villager without a meal, or a wealthy one short of breeding food, buys one emerald's worth from the nearest seller (any time it's awake); during work, a villager holding less than one batch (`count`) of a supply of its trade, with emeralds, walks to the nearest villager within 48 blocks that sells it (`TradeTable.perEmerald`: a `produce` entry or `default_produce`, unlocked at the seller's level) and buys at the seller's player price, as many emeralds' worth as it can afford, the seller can spare (goods minus what it keeps) and fits under the supply's `max`. Goods and emeralds move through `Economy`, so home chests count. First case: the butcher buys wheat from the farmer
- `villager/VillagerNames` — every villager without a custom name gets one from `data/<ns>/villager_names/*.json` (all files merged) when it joins the world; vanilla then shows it on hover and in the trade title ("Jim - Novice")
- `villager/StorageTheft` — any unexplained drop in an owned chest's contents is theft, blamed on whoever placed the hopper or hopper minecart under it. Code that takes items out of a villager's chest must call `StorageTheft.check` before and `recordContents` after, or it will be counted as theft
- `villager/Roads` + `RoadNavigation` + `StrollAlongRoads` — every villager gets a navigation (swapped in on join, via the access transformer on `Mob.navigation`) whose steps cost extra by road tier. Tiers are data: `data/<ns>/villager_road_tiers/*.json` gives a block list (tags `#minenow:villager_roads/tier_1..3`: dirt path; gravel/cobble; bricks/planks/slabs/stairs) and a cost (0.75, 0.4, 0); open ground costs 1.0 and anything cheaper counts as road. Adding a tier is a tag plus a tier file. Idle villagers also stroll to nearby road blocks. Vanilla's PathFinder weights its heuristic by 1.5, so open ground must cost well over 0.5 or detours are never explored. Landing from a jump of 2+ blocks costs 8 for 2 and four times more per extra block (`RoadNavigation.dropCost`, on the landing node, taken once), so villagers take stairs, ramps and ladders down when there are any
- `villager/FollowEmeraldHolder` — unemployed adult villagers follow a player within 10 blocks holding an emerald (not while asleep, trading, panicking or in a raid); led more than 32 blocks from their bed they release it and their meeting bell (vanilla never would) and settle anew
- `farmer/Crops` — what counts as a field crop: any `CropBlock` or `#minecraft:crops` entry (where crop mods register theirs), minus stems and `#minenow:farmer_ignores`; ripe = max age (read from the `age` property for non-CropBlocks); a seed is an item that plants such a crop (or a NeoForge `SpecialPlantable`). Planting checks the crop can survive there
- `farmer/RightClickHarvest` — before breaking a ripe crop the farmer right-clicks it through a NeoForge fake player, so right-click-harvest mods (and crops harvested that way natively) work for villagers; drops spawned at the crop or handed to the fake player go to the farmer. If nothing reacts, the farmer breaks and replants as before. `gametest/SimulatedRightClickHarvest` stands in for such a mod in tests
- `farmer/Field` — recomputed on every scan (when the farmer starts work or runs out of it); a farmer's field is the ground touching a side of its composter (corners don't count) and what connects to it: farmland plus tillable ground (`turns_into_farmland` tag) within vanilla's 4-block watering range, joined through water, surface only, within a range that grows with the farmer's level (novice 10, apprentice 13, journeyman 16, expert 20, master 24). Water is the boundary players build with; anything solid or non-replaceable on top of ground (string, slabs, fences) also cuts it out of the field
- `farmer/` — `TendFields` behaviour (harvest/replant/till its connected field, unload into the farmer's own home chest; needs a hoe, holds it, swings it while reaping for as long as the hoe's mining speed dictates, wears it down, fetches a spare from the home chest; lives in CORE right after `MoveToTargetSink` so standing still to reap isn't undone by WORK's walk back to the job site), `FarmerReport` (`/minenow farmer`: why the nearest farmer is or isn't working: activity, hoe, slots, home chest, field counts), `FarmerWork` (the farmer's WORK package: vanilla's minus `WorkAtComposter` (bread baking, seed composting), `HarvestFarmland` and `UseBonemeal`; TendFields does that work, including bone-mealing young crops), `FarmerRules` (farmers never trample farmland) and its gametests
- `fisherman/` — `FishingWater` (any open surface water within range of the barrel, so barrels can stand on docks: 8 blocks, 3 up or down, the same at every level; shore spots are dry standable blocks beside it) `GoFishing` (walks to the nearest shore spot, casts a bobber, reels in when a fish bites, catch from vanilla's fishing loot table with Luck of the Sea, wears the rod, unloads at its home chest; in CORE like TendFields) and `VillagerBobber` (our own bobber entity, since vanilla's hook needs a player owner: flies, floats, a fish swims towards it with vanilla's V-shaped particle trail, bites; Lure shortens the wait; removes itself when GoFishing stops tending it). `FishermanSkill` is what levels improve: reflexes (a novice sometimes reacts too late and the fish gets away, a master never) and rod care (less wear per catch). `client/VillagerBobberRenderer` draws it with the line to the villager's rod. Trades in `villager_trades/fisherman.json`
- `shepherd/` — `ShearSheep` (shears adult sheep within 8 blocks of the loom, 3 up or down: walks up, holds the sheep still and shears for `ShepherdSkill.shearTicks` (novice 3 s, master 1 s), wool from vanilla's shearing loot table straight into its inventory minus what it tears (novice 40%, master none), unloads at its home chest; in CORE like TendFields). Shepherds open fence gates (`villager/OpenFenceGates`). Trades in `villager_trades/shepherd.json` (shears, any `#minecraft:wool` 18 per emerald)
- `butcher/` — `TendHerd` keeps each kind of livestock (`#minenow:butcher_livestock` entity tag: cow, pig, chicken, rabbit; not sheep) within 8 blocks of the smoker at a steady herd (`ButcherHerd`: young count but are never culled, adults above 6 are culled, never below 2 adults, breeds while under 6 with food it holds); named or leashed animals are pets and never culled. Culls with its axe the way a player fights, from a player's 3-block melee reach, finishing a wounded animal before starting another (`Butchering`: as many swings as the animal's health over the axe's attack damage, one per attack cooldown, each swing wears the axe), so better axes (unlocked by level in the trade file) work faster, like the farmer's hoes; `ButcherDrops` (LivingDropsEvent) puts the drops straight into its inventory plus `ButcherSkill.extraCuts` (0 novice to 2 master, like Looting). Smokes its raw meat in its smoker with coal it holds and takes the cooked meat out (`villager/FurnaceTending`); unloads at its home chest. Trades in `villager_trades/butcher.json` (axes; buys coal and breeding food, never meat; `keeps` holds its raw meat back from unloading for the smoker; sells cooked meat, leather, feathers, rabbit hide/foot)
- `toolsmith/` — the toolsmith (smithing table) crafts what `villager_trades/toolsmith.json` lists under `crafts` (stone/copper/iron/diamond hoes and axes, fishing rods, shears; better tools unlock by level) via the generic `villager/CraftAtWorkstation`: keeps `stock` (default 1) of each in its goods, hammers at its workstation for 5 s per item, materials from its supplies (cobblestone, sticks, string, copper, iron, diamonds). Sells them to players and to villagers: a working villager with no tool of its trade buys the best one it can afford from a crafting villager (`BuyFromVillagers`), so tool money flows back from the farmers and butchers
- `miner/` — a new profession (`Miner`: `minenow:miner`, in `#minecraft:acquirable_job_site`; hard hat outfit in `assets/minenow/textures/entity/(zombie_)villager/profession/miner.png`). Its job site is `FurnaceCartBlock`, a furnace minecart parked off the rails: using a furnace minecart on anything but a rail places it (`Miner.onRightClickBlock`), it's a real furnace (added to vanilla's furnace block entity type), breaks back into the minecart, isn't pathfindable (vanilla treats every non-full-cube block as passable, which sent paths through it), and `client/FurnaceCartRenderer` draws it with vanilla's minecart model (the block model only supplies particles). `Quarry`: the player marks the mine with a closed rim of cobblestone (`#minenow:mine_rim`, all `#c:cobblestones`) flush in the ground, the cart standing on the rim; the cells the rim encloses at that level, within 8 blocks, are the outline (a rim with a gap, or a plain hole, marks nothing, so builds next to a cart are safe). The miner digs the outline layer itself first, standing on the rim, then `DigQuarry` digs the mine laid out by `MineLayout` (pure, unit-tested) down to `MinerSkill.depth` below the cart (8 novice to 40 expert; a master has no limit but bedrock), and no deeper than as many platforms as the strength of a redstone signal into the cart (the shallower of the two wins, so redstone never takes a miner past its level's depth) (`MineLayout.deepestPlatform`; at that platform only its tunnels, not its middle): the shaft layer by layer down to a platform (every 5th layer, keeping a 2-block ring and opening the middle, so a 7x7 shaft leaves a 3x3 hole, with the shaft continuing below); at a platform first the ladder hole, then a 3x3 tunnel out of the middle three columns of each wall at least 5 long, one direction fully before the next (`MinerSkill.tunnelLength`: 8 novice to 24 master; each tunnel line up to the first block it can't dig), then the middle, then on down. The two layers right under a platform are dug together, column by column, so there's never a one-block crawl space under the ring. Where to stand comes from `Quarry.reachable`: a flood from the ladder column through the mine's floored two-high cells at a level; a cell is only worked on when such a spot is within reach (own layer, the two below or the one above, nearest first); each block taking `Mining.ticks` (vanilla's player timing for the pickaxe against the block) with the crack animation, wearing the pickaxe per block; drops go straight into its inventory, ores (`#c:ores`) with up to `MinerSkill.extraOre` extra; blocks its pickaxe can't harvest stay. Before digging it fills, with cobblestone from its stock (it keeps 32), any fluid in or touching the mine near it and any cave opening in the walls, floor or ceiling of the dug mine (`Quarry.sealSpots`), so the mine stays dry and closed and the ladder always has a wall; a block still touching fluid is never dug. It puts a ladder down one shaft wall (a cell beside a solid wall whose top is free ground to step out onto, never on a tunnel wall) as it digs, fills any gap in the ladder first and won't dig a ladder cell it has no ladder for. Vanilla never paths up or down ladders, so `LadderClimb.climbTo` uses the ladder itself: from the surface via the exit beside its top, from inside the mine by stepping onto the column when beside it (a ladder cell over a hole is no place a path can end), then holding itself on the column and moving at ladder speed (vanilla only lifts a mob that keeps pushing into the wall while jumping), sliding down until its feet are at the level, and stepping off onto the floored neighbour cell nearest its spot; then it drops the route planned on the ladder. `DigQuarry` takes the ladder whenever its work is more than a step above or below or past a platform (never dropping through a platform's middle), climbs out to unload or smelt, and `LeaveQuarry` climbs out when work ends or its pickaxe broke. `DigQuarry` re-pins its walk target every tick, since vanilla's work package pulls villagers back within 9 blocks of their job site. It keeps every floor cell of the shaft and tunnels at block light 8 or more (`Quarry.darkSpots`) with wall torches one block above the floor on the outer walls, buying torches or making 4 from a coal and a stick. A cell counts as lit by the light engine or by any light source within reach of 8 by distance (the player's lanterns and glowstone too), since block light updates a moment late and reading it alone put torches side by side. It smelts its raw ore (`#c:raw_materials` only, never its cobblestone) in its furnace minecart plus the nearest furnaces and blast furnaces within 4 blocks that `MinerSkill.extraFurnaces` allows (0 novice to 4 master), spreading the ore in equal shares, through `villager/FurnaceTending` (shared with the butcher's smoker; it adds only the coal a load needs, one per 8 items, and returns to a furnace whose fuel ran out). Trades in `villager_trades/miner.json` (pickaxes by level; buys ladders, torches and sticks; sells cobblestone, coal, ingots, redstone, lapis, diamonds (3 emeralds each) and any `#c:stones`; keeps raw ores, coal and 32 cobblestone)
- `forester/` — a new profession (`Forester`: `minenow:forester`, in `#minecraft:acquirable_job_site`; green felt hat outfit in `assets/minenow/textures/entity/(zombie_)villager/profession/forester.png`). Its job site is the nursery (`NurseryBlock`): potting a sapling into a flower pot that stands on a log (`#minecraft:logs`) makes one instead of vanilla's potted sapling (`Forester.onRightClickBlock`); a pot anywhere else stays vanilla, so potted saplings as decoration are never claimed. Its `tree` state (`NurseryTree`: the nine vanilla tree saplings) is what the forester replants; models reuse vanilla's potted sapling models (with `render_type` cutout), breaking it drops the pot and the sapling, taking the sapling out leaves an empty flower pot, and without the log under it it turns back into vanilla's potted sapling
- `villager/OpenFenceGates` — shepherds and butchers plan paths through closed fence gates (`RoadNavigation` treats them as walkable doors for them), open the gate on their path once right at it (1.2 blocks) and close it as soon as they're clear of the gateway and not about to pass back through; an animal caught in the gateway when it closes is shooed back one block, away from the villager, so animals stay in; other villagers still only open doors
- `villager/ToolCare` — better craftsmen wear their tool less: chance a use wears it, 100% novice down to 40% master (fisherman and shepherd; the farmer's hoe and butcher's axe are unaffected, those trades improve through better tools instead)
- `villager/Tools` — the tool of a villager's trade: shown in hand as a copy while working, worn down by use
- `client/` — `SwingingVillagerRenderer` replaces vanilla's villager renderer so the crossed arms (and held tool) move with swings; registered in `MineNowClient`
- `dev/GametestPlayground` + `PlaygroundLabels` + `client/GametestPlaygroundWorld` — the `runGametestPlayground` run: opens a fresh creative test world and starts the player in a hub with one portal per `gametest/TestGroup` (framed in the group's concrete, labelled with its test and failure counts); each group's district (coloured wool floor and a big sign: Villagers yellow, Home storage red, Roads light blue, Farmer lime, Farmer trading purple, Fisherman cyan, Shepherd white, Butcher brown, Villager trading orange, Toolsmith gray, Miner black, Forester green; layout in `PlaygroundLayout`) sits 1024 blocks from the next, force-loaded so tests tick and labels update, with a portal back to the hub. Tests are idle, each with a floating label (readable name, plus the failure message once it fails; the test block's beacon shows the status) and an overview board with totals and failing tests. `/minenow playground runall` starts them all
- `src/main/resources/data/minenow/test_environment/late_afternoon.json` — a `clock_time` test environment; tests that need a time of day other than work hours use their own environment so they run in a separate batch instead of moving the clock under parallel tests. The environment's clock doesn't take when its batch is the first after server start, so those tests also call `helper.setTime(GameTestHelpers.LATE_AFTERNOON)`; every test that depends on the time of day must set it, since it otherwise inherits whatever the previous batch left
- `gametest/` — shared gametest helpers (`fence` lays the stone floor and barrier walls; spawning employed/housed villagers). The test structure's own floor doesn't load, so every test must call `fence` first
- Every gametest is registered in `MineNow` with a `TestGroup`; give new tests the group of the feature they test, or add a group (title, wool floor, concrete portal frame) for a new feature
- `src/main/resources/data/minenow/test_instance/` — one JSON per gametest; `structure/field_24x6x24.nbt` is the empty stone-floored test space

## Dev-only mods

`build.gradle`'s `clientDevMods` configuration (Right Click Harvest + JamLib) is added only to the client run's classpath, for trying compatibility by hand. Never use `runtimeOnly` for such mods: that loads them in `runGameTestServer` too, which changes behaviour under test and makes CI depend on Modrinth.

## Workflow

- **TDD**: write a failing test first, then implement — a GameTest if the behavior needs a live `Level`
  (blocks, entities, ticking), a plain JUnit test under `src/test/java` only if it's pure logic that touches no
  items: on 26.3 item components are bound when datapacks load, so `new ItemStack(...)` throws "Components not bound
  yet" in unit tests. Anything with items is a gametest (they can be instant, see `better_hoes_reap_faster`). See
  `feedback_tdd_workflow` memory.
- **Verify with GameTests, not just compile**: `./gradlew runGameTestServer` — a green compile can still hide a broken tag/behavior. Don't declare a task done on compile alone. For pure-logic changes covered by `src/test/java`, `./gradlew test` is the equivalent gate.
- `@GameTest` annotations don't register on this build; tests are wired via `RegisterEvent` + JSON (see `reference_forge_gametest_setup` memory).
- Custom block tags live in `tags/block/` (singular), not `tags/blocks/` — wrong folder fails silently.
- ARGB colors: always include the alpha byte — omitting it renders fully transparent.
- Villager AI: add `Behavior<Villager>`s to vanilla brain activities via `BrainHook`; no mixins unless a vanilla hook is truly missing. To drop a vanilla behaviour, rebuild that profession's brain from `VillagerGoalPackages` the way `FarmerWork` does (only on a fresh brain).
- Access transformers live in `META-INF/accesstransformer.cfg` (declared in `neoforge.mods.toml`); prefer one over a mixin when only visibility is in the way.
- `PathNavigation.createPath` returns null until the mob is on the ground (and never with NoAI); compute paths a few ticks after spawning in tests.
- On 26.3 iron golem spawning only needs villagers that slept recently; `LAST_WORKED_AT_POI` is written by vanilla but never read and not even registered.
- Per-villager state that must survive reloads goes in a NeoForge data attachment, not a custom brain `MemoryModuleType`: the brain drops saved memories that aren't registered at rebuild time, and ours are only added afterwards.

## Token-cost hygiene (read before running builds/tests)

- Compile before running gametests: `./gradlew compileJava` first, `runGameTestServer` only once that's clean. A compile error inside a full gametest run produces far more noisy output than catching it standalone.
- Always add `--console=plain -q` to gradle invocations to suppress Minecraft/Forge startup spam (asset indexing, mod discovery), e.g. `./gradlew runGameTestServer --console=plain -q 2>&1 | tail -80`.
- Never dump raw `./gradlew runGameTestServer` / build output into context. Pipe through `grep`/`tail` for the failure only, e.g. `./gradlew runGameTestServer --console=plain -q 2>&1 | tail -80` or `| grep -A20 FAILED`.
- Don't `cat`/`Read` full log files under `run/logs/`, `run/crash-reports/`, or `build/`; grep for the specific error first. (These are also permission-denied for `Read` in `.claude/settings.json` — use `grep`/`tail` via Bash instead.)
- For "find X" or "where is Y defined" questions, use the `Explore` agent instead of reading many files directly in the main conversation.
- Prefer `Edit` over re-reading a file you just wrote to confirm it — the tool already errors on failure.

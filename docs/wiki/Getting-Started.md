# Getting Started

## Requirements

- Minecraft 26.3
- NeoForge 26.3 or newer

Drop the jar into your `mods` folder. It also works on a server. Speech bubbles, the village border outline, the custom villager rendering and the settings screen are client features, so they only show for players who have the mod installed.

## The idea

Vanilla already gives villagers beds, workstations, breeding and schedules. Hearthfolk adds behaviour on top of those. If a villager has a bed and a workstation, it works. If it is missing something, it tells you (see [Troubleshooting](Troubleshooting)).

Villagers also level up by working, not only by trading with you, and their trades are fixed prices you can learn instead of random cycling offers.

## Your first village

1. Give each villager a bed and a workstation, as in vanilla. Add a bell so they have a meeting point. The bell also makes the group of buildings a village.
2. Put a chest or barrel within 8 blocks of each bed. The villager claims it as its home chest and locks it for players. Right-click it to see whose it is.
3. Build the workplace for the job (see [Professions](Professions)). A farmer needs a composter beside a field, a miner needs a cobblestone rim and a furnace minecart, and so on.
4. Hand over starting gear if the villager has none. Most jobs need a tool: throw it near the villager or trade it over.
5. Wait. A villager with an emerald-rich purse and food buys breeding food and has children, so the village grows.

## Seeing what is going on

Hold the Villager Inspector (creative tab Tools and Utilities) and right-click a villager for a report book. `/minenow villager` prints the same for the nearest villager. A villager shaking its head, or showing a speech bubble with an item, is telling you what it needs.

## Playing with the settings

Open the mod list, pick Hearthfolk and press Config. In single player this edits the world you are in. See [Settings](Settings).
